  NOTE: f64 NEON is SLOWER than f64 scalar. The A53 NEON datapath is 64-bit,
  so a 128-bit double vector op is cracked into two micro-ops and gains
  nothing. f32 NEON does speed up, which confirms this is the datapath
  width and not a benchmark artefact. -O3 auto-vectorising our double
  maths into fadd v.2d is therefore free of benefit, not free of cost.

  Per control mode (the switch in Update()), RBDL portion only:
  eGravityCompensation            5.6 us/cycle    0.6% of the 1000 us budget
  eComputedTorque                11.1 us/cycle    1.1% of the 1000 us budget
  eFullDynamics                  16.6 us/cycle    1.7% of the 1000 us budget
  eTrackingServo                 23.4 us/cycle    2.3% of the 1000 us budget

  All of these are dense double-precision linear algebra (Eigen + RBDL);
  the arithmetic is what costs the time, not the I/O.

  Two different denominators, do not confuse them:
    vs the 1 ms DEADLINE      -> 2.3%, i.e. the loop finishes with room to spare
    vs the loop's OWN runtime -> a substantial share of what the loop does
  The second one is NOT quantified here: the figures above are means, while
  the only RT loop timings we have (D10 A/B: max 33.9 us non-isolated /
  51.1 us isolated) are maxima including jitter. Dividing a mean by a max
  would overstate it. Establishing the share needs a mean loop time, which
  requires the robot running.

  Break-even, from the measured mean: FP would have to get 43x slower
  (23.4 us -> 1000 us) to threaten the budget. Whether software emulation
  crosses that line cannot be measured on this board — AArch64 has no
  soft-float ABI and libgcc ships no __adddf3/__muldf3/__divdf3 to call.
